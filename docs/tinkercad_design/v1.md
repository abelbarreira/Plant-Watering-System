1. Wiring review

The topology is sound. I traced every wire in the PDF, and the connections are:

Signal	Connection
HC-SR04 ECHO	P0 (TRIG and ECHO cross without a junction dot, so ECHO is P0 and TRIG is P1)
HC-SR04 TRIG	P1
SEN0308 SIG	P2 (analog)
Pump gate	P8 → R2 100 Ω → MOSFET gate, with R1 10 kΩ gate-to-GND
Pump	12 V (P1+) → motor → MOSFET drain, source → GND
Flyback diode D1	Cathode on +12 V, anode on drain (correct orientation)
Sensors	Both powered from the micro:bit 3.3 V pin
Grounds	micro:bit GND, sensor GNDs and PSU negative on one common bus

The pump is a low-side switch with a gate resistor, a pull-down and a flyback diode, which is the right design. The pull-down keeps the pump off while the micro:bit boots. All four pins you chose are free of the LED matrix.

What would bite you on real hardware (Tinkercad hides these):

MOSFET: Use a logic-level part such as an IRLZ44N or IRLB8721. The common IRF520, IRF540 and IRF3205 are not fully on at 3.3 V, so they run hot and starve the pump.
HC-SR04 at 3.3 V: It is a 5 V part and is often unreliable at 3.3 V. Use a 3.3 V-compatible one (HC-SR04P or RCWL-1601), or power it from 5 V and put a 1k/2k divider on ECHO, since the micro:bit pins are not 5 V tolerant.
Powering the micro:bit: The schematic doesn’t show it, and 12 V can’t go into the micro:bit. Use a 12 V→5 V buck converter into a USB cable, or a separate battery pack. Keep the common ground.
Pump voltage: Make sure the supply matches the pump’s rating. The BOM says “12 , 5”, which I read as 12 V / 5 A. Many small peristaltic pumps are 6 V or 12 V, so check the pump label. Also confirm it is a 2-wire brushed DC pump, not a stepper pump.
Pump noise: Add a 100 nF ceramic capacitor across the pump terminals and about 220 µF across the 12 V rail near the MOSFET. Brushed motors can glitch the ADC and the ultrasonic timing.
Diode part: A 1N4007 or 1N5819 works for D1.

Worth adding: A cheap float switch as a hardware backup for the low-water check, since ultrasonic sensors can be fooled by splashes and condensation. Also make sure the pot drains, because 500 ml in one go can overflow a small saucer.

2. Code

plant_watering.py is written against the Tinkercad micro:bit Python API. I ran its logic against a simulated clock, and these cases behaved as intended:

The pump stays off at boot.
Moist soil never triggers a run.
Dry soil with a low tank never starts the pump.
Dry soil with a good tank runs exactly once for the set time, and the 30-minute cooldown blocks the next run.
A tank that drains mid-run cuts the pump early.
A missing echo means no pumping.
A+B stops the pump.

Safety features beyond what you asked for:

The soil must read dry three times in a row before watering.
The cooldown gives water time to soak in. Capacitive sensors respond slowly, and without it the pump can loop.
If the ultrasonic reading is invalid, the pump does not start.

Before it works you must set three values (the file has placeholders):

Soil calibration: Press button A to see the raw soil reading, once in air or dry soil and once in water, and enter them as SOIL_RAW_DRY and SOIL_RAW_WET.
Reservoir limit: Fill the tank to the lowest level you accept (one 500 ml dose plus a little reserve), press B, and enter that distance as LOW_WATER_CM.
Pump time: Time the pump into a measuring jug and set PUMP_RUN_SECONDS to deliver about 500 ml.
